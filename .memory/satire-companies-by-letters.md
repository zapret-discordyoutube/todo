In the "sovereign hosting" satire of 2026-11-03 (`DPI/sovereign-hosting-wipe-november-2026.md`) the owner asked to name the hosting providers by letters, not by name. When asked, she chose: letters in the text, while inline links and the Sources list still point to the companies' channels and media (a curious reader can see who is who). Tone: "patriotic satire throughout", and she added that it must still be a real, useful technical article ("what this can lead to"), not a pamphlet.

**Why:** the joke works without naming and keeps the article from reading as an attack on one company, while the vault rule "every source is a clickable link" stays intact.

**How to apply:** in such articles, letters for hosting providers only (victims like banks, retailers, agencies and product vendors such as VMmanager may be named); images get generic source lines ("official statements of the companies") instead of company names; one letter per company in an article, avoid clashes. Every satirical statement still needs a fact with a link; motives and causal links are hedged in the sentence. Related: [[sources-as-inline-links-plus-list]], [[article-deliverables]].
